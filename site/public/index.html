Search here, or run your own

Search right here on plumbsearch.org. If you'd rather keep your searches to yourself, run your own copy on a desktop or home server; it's optional.

Sites, not pages

For each site Plumb keeps its name, homepage title and description, and what other sites call it, plus its country and kind from Wikidata. So “banks” lists real banks, and you can pick your country.

Search across the Plumb network

Plumb nodes share crawls and can ask each other for results. Desktop and Docker join the network by default. Use the search settings to include network results.

Private and meaning search

Search privately in your browser so your query stays on your device, or find sites by topic with meaning search. Private search uses WebAssembly in your browser; ordinary search works without JavaScript.

Get Plumb

Server or homelab

One container for Linux on x86 or ARM, including a 64-bit Raspberry Pi. The search page is on port 8080.

docker run -d --name plumb --init \
  --restart unless-stopped \
  -p 8080:8080 -v plumb-data:/data \
  ghcr.io/sueheir/plumb-search:latest

Docker guide: settings, updates and backups.

Desktop app

For Windows, macOS (Apple silicon and Intel) and Linux (.deb, .rpm and AppImage). Its window controls your node; searches and results open in your usual browser.

Build the desktop app

Installers are not published yet. Check future releases, or use Docker today.

Make it your browser's search engine. Open your Plumb page in Firefox, right-click the address bar and choose “Add Plumb Search”. Other browsers can add it by hand with the search address http://127.0.0.1:7586/search?q=%s for the desktop app, or your server's address followed by /search?q=%s.

For site owners

Plumb visits homepages to fill in each site's title and to find new sites through its links. Its crawler:

  • identifies itself as PlumbSearch/<version> (+https://github.com/SueHeir/plumb-search),
  • obeys robots.txt, including Crawl-delay,
  • fetches one page per site: the homepage.

Every copy of Plumb does its own crawling, so visits can come from many addresses, all following the same rules. To keep Plumb out, add this to your robots.txt:

User-agent: PlumbSearch
Disallow: /

Questions or problems? Open an issue on GitHub.

Where it's going

Plumb is a working prototype with local and network search, shared crawling, private browser search, and optional anonymous popularity sharing. Work continues on reliability, coverage, and easier installation. No accounts and no ads.

  1. One machine. Build an index from public data and put the official site first.
  2. Network search. Nodes share crawls and ask one another for results.
  3. Community crawling. Copies take turns checking sites, so the index stays fresh on its own.
  4. Private popularity. Count what people pick without tracking anyone, and keep bots from gaming it.
